Revision Notes

A chemical reaction

A chemical reaction is a process in which original substance(s) loses its nature and identity and forms new substance(s) with different properties. Chemical reactions involve the breaking of chemical bonds in reacting substances and making of new chemical bonds to produce new substances through atomic rearrangement.

  • Breaking of the chemical bonds and formation of new chemical bonds is responsible for the occurrence of a chemical reaction.
  • The substances which take part in a chemical reaction are called Reactants.
  • The substances which are formed in a chemical reaction are called Products.

Examples of chemical reaction:

  1. Digestion of food
  2. Respiration
  3. Rusting of iron
  4. Burning of magnesium ribbon
  5. Formation of curd

A chemical reaction can be identified by either of the following observations:

S. No. Characteristics Examples
1. Change in state The combustion reaction of candle wax is characterized by a change in state from solid to liquid and gas.
2. Change in colour The chemical reaction between citric acid and purple coloured potassium permanganate solution is characterized by a change in colour from purple to colourless.
3. Evolution of gas The chemical reaction between zinc and dilute sulphuric acid is characterized by hydrogen gas.
Zn(s) + H2SO4(aq) ZnSO4(aq) + H2(g)
4. Change in temperature The reaction between quicklime and water to form slaked lime is characterized by an increase in temperature.
5. Formation of a precipitate When an aqueous solution of sodium sulphate is mixed with the aqueous solution of barium chloride, barium sulphate comes in the form of white precipitate.
Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) BaSO4(s) + 2NaCl(aq)

Burning of Magnesium Ribbon:
  • Question Asked in Board Exams: Why is a magnesium ribbon cleaned with sandpaper before burning in air?
  • Answer: Magnesium is a reactive metal. When exposed to air, it forms a protective layer of Magnesium Oxide (MgO) or Basic Magnesium Carbonate on its surface. This layer prevents further reaction with oxygen. Sandpaper removes this layer so it burns smoothly.
  • Observation: Burns with a dazzling white flame and changes into a white powder of Magnesium Oxide (2Mg + O2 ΔIgnition 2MgO).
  • Safety Note: Wear suitable eyeglasses because the dazzling white light can damage eyes.

Chemical equations

A chemical equation is the symbolic representation of a chemical reaction in the form of symbols and formulae. It is a way to represent the chemical reaction in a concise and informative way.

For example,
Magnesium + Oxygen Magnesium oxide
(Reactants) (Product)

This equation is called a word equation. The word equation can be written into a chemical equation by writing symbols and formulae of the substance in place of their name.

2Mg + O2 Δ 2MgO

Information Conveyed by a Balanced Chemical Equation

A balanced chemical equation provides two distinct types of information:

  1. Qualitative Information:
    • The names of the reactants taking part in the chemical reaction.
    • The names of the products formed in the chemical reaction.
  2. Quantitative Information:
    • The relative number of atoms or molecules of reactants and products taking part.
    • The relative number of moles of each substance involved.
    • The mass of each substance taking part in or produced by the reaction.
    • Mass-mass, mass-volume, or volume-volume relationships between reactants and products.

Writing a chemical equation

  1. The symbols of elements and the formulae of reacting substances (reactants) are written on the left hand side of the equation, with a plus (+) sign between them.
  2. The symbols and formulae of the substances formed (products) are written on the right hand side of the equation, with a plus sign (+) between them.
  3. An arrow sign () is put between the reactants and the products.
  4. The physical states of the reactants and products are also mentioned in a chemical equation.

Skeletal chemical equation: A chemical equation which simply represents the symbols and formulas of reactants and products taking part in the reaction is known as skeletal chemical equation for a reaction.
For example: For the burning of Magnesium in the air, Mg + O2 MgO is the skeletal equation.

Balanced Equation: The equation in which atoms of various elements on both sides of a chemical equation are equal in accordance with the law of conservation of mass.

The example of balanced chemical equation :

  1. CO(g) + 2H2(g) 340 atm300°C / ZnO+CrO CH3OH(l)
  2. 6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l) SunlightChlorophyll C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g) (Glucose)

The process of equalizing the atoms of various elements both on either sides of an equation is called the balancing of chemical equation. Methods include the Hit and Trial (Inspection) method and Partial Equation method. Let us understand this with the help of an example given below:

Key Word: Law of conservation of mass
It states that, "The matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. "OR"
the total mass of reactants = total mass of products". Balancing ensures material balance/mass balance so that no atoms are lost or gained.

Types of Chemical Reactions

I. Combination Reaction: The reaction in which two or more reactants combine to form a single product.

e.g., (i) Burning of coal: C(s) + O2(g) ΔIgnition CO2(g) + 393.3 kJ (12g pure carbon burning in excess air releases 393.3 kJ of heat)

(ii) Formation of water: 2H2(g) + O2(g) Spark25°C 2H2O(l)

(iii) CaO(s) + H2O(l) ExothermicRoom Temp Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat (Quick lime Slaked lime)

(iv) SO2(g) + O2(g) SO3(g) (Sulphur dioxide + Oxygen Sulphur trioxide)

(v) CaO(s) + CO2(g) CaCO3(s) (Calcium oxide + Carbon dioxide Calcium carbonate)

Important Board Question on Slaked Lime Solution:
A solution of slaked lime Ca(OH)2 is used for white-washing walls. Calcium hydroxide reacts slowly with the carbon dioxide in air to form a thin layer of calcium carbonate on the walls, giving a shiny finish to the walls after 2 to 3 days.
Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) Slow2-3 Days CaCO3(s) + H2O(l) (Marble has the same formula: CaCO3).

Exothermic Reactions: Reaction in which heat is liberated/released along with formation of products.

e.g., (i) Burning of natural gas: CH4(g) + 2O2(g) ΔIgnition CO2(g) + 2H2O(g) + Heat

(ii) Respiration is also an exothermic reaction:
C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g) Enzymes37°C 6CO2(aq) + 6H2O(l) + energy (Glucose)

(iii) Burning of Carbon: C(s) + O2(g) CO2(g) + Heat (393.3 kJ)

(iv) Decomposition of vegetable matter into compost is also an example of an exothermic reaction.

VI. Oxidation and Reduction (Redox Reactions):

  • Oxidation: Addition of oxygen or electronegative element OR removal of hydrogen or electropositive element OR loss of electrons.
    Examples:
    - Addition of Oxygen: S + O2 SO2
    - Addition of Electronegative Element: 2FeCl2 + Cl2 2FeCl3
    - Removal of Hydrogen: H2S + Cl2 2HCl + S
    - Removal of Electropositive Element: 2KI + H2O2 I2 + 2KOH
    - Heating copper: 2Cu + O2 Δ (Heat)Air 2CuO
  • Reduction: Addition of hydrogen or electropositive element OR removal of oxygen or electronegative element OR gain of electrons.
    Examples:
    - Addition of Hydrogen: H2 + Cl2 2HCl
    - Addition of Electropositive Element: 2HgCl2 + SnCl2 Hg2Cl2 + SnCl4
    - Removal of Oxygen: ZnO + C Zn + CO
    - Removal of Electronegative Element: 2FeCl3 + H2 2FeCl2 + 2HCl
    - Reduction of CuO: CuO + H2 Δ (Heat)Reduction Cu + H2O
  • Oxidising Agent: The substance which gives oxygen/electronegative element or removes hydrogen/electropositive element (gets reduced itself).
  • Reducing Agent: The substance which gives hydrogen/electropositive element or removes oxygen/electronegative element (gets oxidised itself).

Effects of oxidation reactions in everyday life:

1. Corrosion: Corrosion is defined as the chemical process of slow destruction / eating up of metal surfaces when exposed to air, moisture, and chemicals over time. The corrosion of metals involves transfer of electrons from the metal to oxygen through water.

Detailed Chemical Mechanism of Iron Corrosion (Rusting):

  • Oxidation Step: Iron atom loses electrons and oxidizes to Iron (III) ions:
    Fe Oxidation Fe3+ + 3e-
  • Reduction Step: Oxygen gas in presence of moisture gains electrons and reduces to oxide ion:
    O2 + 4e- Reduction 2O2-
  • Compound Formation: Iron (III) ions and oxide ions combine to form Iron (III) Oxide:
    2Fe3+ + 3O2- Fe2O3
    Also, Iron (III) ions react with hydroxide ions from moisture to form Iron (III) Hydroxide:
    Fe3+ + 3OH- Fe(OH)3

Conditions Necessary for Corrosion:
(i) Presence of oxygen in the air.
(ii) Presence of moisture or water in the air.
Note: Rate of corrosion increases if two metals are in contact or if air contains acidic pollutant gases like SO2, NO2, CO2.

Examples:
(a) Rusting of iron (reddish brown powder).
(b) Green coating on Copper articles (Basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2).
(c) Black coating on Silver articles (Silver sulphide Ag2S).

Effects of corrosion:
(a) Damage to ships, car bodies, bridges, railings, railway tracks, and buildings.
(b) Wasteful process leading to financial losses and metal destruction.

Prevention of Rusting/Corrosion:
(a) Coating with paint, varnish, or grease.
(b) Applying a thin layer of oil over metal surface.
(c) Coating thick layer of corrosion resistant metal like Zinc (Galvanisation), Tin, or Chromium.
(d) Alloying with other metals like chromium and nickel (e.g., Stainless Steel).

2. Rancidity: Rancidity is the process of slow oxidation of oils and fats present in food materials resulting in volatile bad smelling compounds causing foul odour and stale taste (common in cheese, curd, oily snacks left for long periods, especially in summer).

Rancidity can be prevented by the following ways:
(a) Addition of food additives called Anti-oxidants (e.g., BHA - Butylated Hydroxyanisole, BHT) to check oxidation.
(b) Storing food in air-tight containers to prevent oxygen access.
(c) Storing food in refrigerator (low temperature slows reaction rate).
(d) Flushing potato chip bags with unreactive gas like Nitrogen (N2) to exclude oxygen.

Q5 (Page 10): Why is the amount of gas collected in one of the test tubes in Activity 1.7 (electrolysis of water) double of the amount collected in the other? Name this gas.

Ans: Water (H2O) consists of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom by volume. During electrolysis, water decomposes into hydrogen and oxygen in a 2:1 ratio by volume. The gas collected in double volume at the cathode is Hydrogen gas (H2).
